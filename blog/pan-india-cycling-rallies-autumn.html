Upcoming Pan-India Cycling Rallies: Where to Ride This Autumn

Here's the honest picture: July through September is India's monsoon window, and organized cycling events genuinely thin out during these months — most calendars pick back up from October onward. If you're looking for something to sign up for right now, the smarter move is treating this season as build-up training for what's coming in autumn and winter, rather than expecting a packed September calendar.

Here's the honest picture: July through September is India's monsoon window, and organized cycling events genuinely thin out during these months — most calendars pick back up from October onward. If you're looking for something to sign up for right now, the smarter move is treating this season as build-up training for what's coming in autumn and winter, rather than expecting a packed September calendar.

What's realistically on the calendar

Tour of Nilgiris (September–November) — A long-running, multi-day stage tour through the Nilgiri hills of Tamil Nadu and Kerala, known for its climbing and camaraderie-driven format rather than pure racing speed. If you want a genuine autumn target, this is one of the more established options, with editions typically running across the September-to-November window depending on the year's schedule.

IRONMAN 70.3 Goa (October) — Not a pure cycling rally, but the 90 km bike leg of this half-distance triathlon draws a serious cycling crowd training specifically for it through September. If you're already triathlon-curious, this is a strong reason to keep your bike fitness building right now.

Audax India Brevets (year-round, 12 chapters) — If you want something to actually ride *this month*, Audax brevets are the reliable fallback. These are self-supported, timed long-distance rides (200 km up to 1200 km) run by regional chapters across the country pretty much continuously, monsoon included. They're less "rally," more personal-challenge format, but they keep you honest on saddle time when bigger organized events are scarce.

Regional sportives and gran fondos — Cities like Mumbai, Pune, Bangalore, Delhi, Hyderabad, Kolkata, and Chennai each run local gran fondos and sportives, but these cluster more heavily from October through February, once the weather cooperates. Keep an eye on your city's cycling club calendar for announcements — most confirm dates only 6–8 weeks out.

Worth circling for later in the year

If you're planning your autumn-into-winter season now, these are the confirmed anchors:

  • Bajaj Pune Grand Tour — India's first UCI-sanctioned stage race, run in January. If you're building serious form, this is the marquee target on the domestic calendar, though it's a pro-athlete category event rather than open registration for most riders.
  • Tour of Thekkady — A Kerala stage race, typically February–March.
  • IRONMAN 70.3 Goa — October, as above.

How to use the monsoon months well

Since the rally calendar is thin right now, this is genuinely a good stretch to:

  • Build base mileage indoors or on covered routes when monsoon rain makes outdoor riding unsafe.
  • Work on bike-handling skills in controlled conditions — cornering, braking, group-riding position — so you're sharper once the October–February season opens up.
  • Register early for October–November events once dates are confirmed; the Tour of Nilgiris and regional gran fondos tend to fill up once monsoon lifts and everyone rushes to sign up at once.

If you've got a specific city or state in mind, local cycling federation chapters and Audax India's chapter pages are the most reliable place to check for last-minute additions to the calendar as autumn progresses.
